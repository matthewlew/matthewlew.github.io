System Philosophy 02 / 06

The 80/90 Rule.

How do you summarize your core value proposition as a Design Systems Architect?

"I build the structural engines and layout guardrails that allow product teams to scale without creating design or engineering debt. Instead of just drawing components, I design the token architectures, navigation frameworks, and AI context layers that bridge the gap between Figma and code."

Why is the "80/90 Rule" critical for modern product teams?

"Startups often burn months trying to build custom, component-heavy libraries from scratch before they even know their core product patterns—leading directly to system debt. My approach focuses on using native, out-of-the-box primitives (like shadcn/ui) to cover 80% to 90% of operational workflows immediately. By establishing clear layout rules and semantic token boundaries first, product teams get 10x velocity without the months of upfront engineering burn."

Why is an AI-ready design system more important now than ever?

"In the age of AI tools like Claude Code, if your system lacks structured context sheets or token guardrails, AI will generate code slop and fragmented UI. An AI-ready design system acts as the ultimate prompt layer: you give the LLM strict layout parameters, semantic tokens, and component boundaries, and it generates high-fidelity, production-aligned UI every single time."

How do you solve brand-to-product drift?

"Brand marketing and product design often operate in silos. At DoorDash, when the team selected a new brand red, the color was technically inaccessible for digital UI standards. I created a digital-compliant version of DoorDash red that balanced visual identity with strict accessibility contrast ratios—a token that is still in production today. Design systems shouldn't just enforce layout rules—they should protect and express brand identity consistently."

Why is iconography the "last frontier", and how does Open Icons solve it?

"At DoorDash, I manually designed a 1,000+ icon library. It taught me that manual vector craftsmanship doesn't scale in high-velocity organizations. Open Icons is designed to treat iconography as a dynamic, parametric system. By defining underlying design rules—stroke weight axes, optical sizing, corner radii—we turn a historical design bottleneck into an open engine where AI generation tools can create new, contextually aligned icons on demand."

What is your prediction for the next big horizon in design systems?

"We are heading toward multi-modal, agentic design systems where traditional GUIs might not even be the primary output. The ultimate design system won't just feed pixels to a screen; it will streamline content and structural parameters so human users and AI agents stay in complete sync. The underlying design system will serve as the single source of truth for both human and machine interaction."
