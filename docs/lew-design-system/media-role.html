emph-media — chrome that sits on artwork

Every other emphasis in LDS resolves against a surface colour that is known when you write the CSS. This one does not. It is for controls layered over a photo, a video still, album art, or a generated gradient — surfaces whose colour is decided at runtime and changes as the user scrolls.

The whole API is one decision color

Set the ink. Surface, border, hover and pressed states are all color-mix derivations of it, so they follow automatically: 

<div class="lds-card emph-media" style="color:#fff">…</div>

<button class="lds-btn lds-btn--icon emph-media">★</button>
<button class="lds-chip emph-media lds-chip--selected">Linear</button>

The role is stroke-defined, not fill-defined: at rest there is no fill at all, just a 1px ink stroke at 60%. That is deliberate and measured — see the media surface study. In short, an alpha fill over artwork is just a dimmer: it moves lightness only, so it desaturates the very thing it sits on (a 30% fill costs ~36% of the backdrop's chroma), and because the fill is the same ink as the label, every percent of fill also eats label contrast. Dropping it fixes both, and a 1px stroke can carry the control on its own.

Why the stroke is 60%, not the usual 28%

An ink-alpha mix moves mostly chroma, not luminance, so on saturated artwork a faint hairline simply matches its backdrop — a white 28% stroke over pure red is pink-on-red, measuring Lc 0.0 on red, blue and magenta. 40% still fails magenta (7.8), 50% still fails it (13.6). 60% is the first value where every backdrop tested clears the Lc 15 non-text floor.

What this replaces

Old fixed .lds-glass

The previous .lds-glass hardcoded rgba(255,255,255,.14) fill and rgba(255,255,255,.28) border, so it dissolved on any light backdrop. It now paints the media tokens and adapts, falling back to the old recipe only when used without emph-media.

Mode does not flip it

.mode-dark deliberately has no effect on media chrome. Media chrome is backdrop-relative, not mode-relative — a white pill over a bright photo is equally wrong in light mode and dark mode. What changes is the ink, and the ink comes from the backdrop.

Choosing the ink lew-design-system/ink

The role handles everything downstream of the ink; picking the ink is a sampling problem — read the backdrop at the element's position, choose the most legible foreground. That ships separately as lew-design-system/ink, which resolves it against APCA, verified against the apca-w3 reference across a 3,456-pair gamut sweep. 

import { bestInkOn, inkOn, Lc } from 'lew-design-system/ink'

// zero-config knockout
el.style.color = inkOn(backdropHex)

// or prefer your own brand colours, falling back only when none is legible
const { hex } = bestInkOn(backdropHex, paletteStops, Lc.BODY_LARGE)

Two things worth knowing before you enforce a floor. On mid-tone and saturated backdrops the Lc 75 body floor is physically unreachable by any ink — take the best available rather than failing. And enforcing it naively destroys on-brand colour: in palette only ~6% of stops clear it outright, so ~94% of labels collapse to flat white or black. Walking the chosen colour along its OKLCH lightness axis with hue held is what keeps it — and that walk needs real gamut mapping, or per-channel clamping shifts the hue by ~29°.

For backdrops whose tone you already know, emph-media--on-dark / --on-light skip sampling entirely.
